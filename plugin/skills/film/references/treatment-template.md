# Treatment template

The director writes `treatment.md` with these sections in this order. Every section is a decision; write numbers
where they exist (millimetres, watts, f-stops, seconds). The worked example is a release-history film made as
paper models on a desk: a paper lantern rolled from a sheet sets off down a road laid only where a release
happened, one light becomes three and then five, and at dawn they stop at bare desk.

```markdown
# <working title>: treatment

## Premise
Three loglines, one sentence each. Strike the most obvious one and say in a line why it was obvious.
The chosen logline.

## Genre and tone
One line each.

## Central metaphor
What stands for what (a lantern is a model line; the road exists only where a release happened; a ribbon's
length is its context window, to scale). Each mapping must be true to the facts, so the staging cannot lie.

## Cast
Original characters only. For each: what it is made of, its size, how it moves (its weight follows from its
material), what changes about it during the film and which fact each change carries.

## Look: pitches
Six or more pitches, one paragraph each, that differ in medium. For each: the medium, the world, the palette
family, the light, and the one image that would be the poster.

## Look: the choice
Which pitch and why, argued from the premise, the audience and the owner's words. The runner-up. If the best pitch
needs something this setup lacks, say what and which pitch you chose instead.
Ledger: the last five entries' medium and palette family, and how this film differs (or "series of <entry>",
when the owner asked for one).

## The maker (when the look should read as made by a person)
The five answers of handmade.md, section 1: who made it, with what, on what surface at what size, lit by what,
and which marks of making are in frame.

## World, palette, type, materials, light, camera
The set and its scale; the palette as it follows from the materials and the light; the type faces for labels
and for words inside the picture (check digits and capitals of a display face: a handwriting face once drew
"1M" as "IM"); the lens, f-stop and focus rule; the camera language (locked off, hand-pushed slides, cuts on
landmarks); the pose rate (12 a second for stop motion).

## Formats
Each format the film ships in. A vertical version is a framing of its own: a camera per format in the plan and
plates of its own, not a crop of the wide plates.

## Signature shot (the sample)
The shot that best shows the film, pose by pose: poses, camera, beats with their pose numbers, labels with their
exact words and poses, sound cues. This is built first and shown to the owner.

## Story beats and facts
| Beat | Shot(s) | Fact it carries | Source in content.json |

## Sound
The palette, the intensity curve by moment, the cue kinds and what each marks.

## Intro and outro
What opens and closes the film (from the profile's habits if it has them), and whether each is a Blender shot or
a hold with composited text.

## The last image
What is on the final frame and how long it holds.

## Budget
Tokens by step and render minutes (poses x measured or assumed seconds per pose). The owner's cap if he gave one.

## Risks and what to cut first
The parts most likely to fail or run long, the fallback for each, and the order in which beats are dropped.

## Owner
His words on the sample, with the date. Filled in at step 4.
```

## Rules for the pitches

- Spread out before choosing. Media to start from: drawn, painted, cut paper, stop-motion, miniature, stylised
  3D, realistic 3D, photographic, typographic, pixel, wool and felt, carved relief, glass and light, ink wash,
  blueprint. This is a prompt to spread out, not a menu; a pitch outside it is welcome.
- Pitches differ in medium, not only in colour. Two pitches in the same medium count as one.
- Do not write which looks to avoid, and do not carry one in from an earlier film. A list of things to avoid is
  an anchor.
- Choose by fit and say why. Ease of building is not an argument; what the premise needs is.
- The owner's words decide over the pitches, the profile and the ledger. "Same as last time", a reference image,
  "realistic": take it, record it, and pitch inside it (six variations of that direction).
- A named studio, artist or film given as a reference becomes a list of traits (painted backgrounds, soft
  natural light, limited animation, hand-cut edges). Apply the traits to original characters and scenes. Do not
  reproduce their characters, frames or designs, and do not name them in the film.

## Worked example: the pitch list of the first film

1. Paper lanterns at blue hour: a card-and-paper diorama at dusk; the traveller is a walking paper lantern.
2. Ink scroll: wash on silk, the road one brush stroke, the traveller a red seal dot.
3. Carved relief: chiselled plywood layers side-lit like a museum model; the traveller a brass ball in a groove.
4. Glass and neon night: a black void, the road a circuit of light tubes.
5. Blueprint survey: white line work on deep blue, releases as survey stations.
6. Wool and felt stop-motion: felted hills, knitted road, a yarn-ball traveller.

Chosen: 1, because a light-carrying traveller turns every release into a visible change in the frame's own light,
and a dark, calm world leaves room for labels. The first clean render of it was called "generic"; the owner
accepted it once it became paper craft on a desk with tools, tape and imperfect cuts in frame. The pitch was
right and the finish was not: the sample step exists to find that out on one shot.
